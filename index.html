    Methodology

    How We Calculate Your Budget

    Every figure the calculator shows is derived from your inputs by three published formulas: a budget allocation model, a cost-per-hour model, and a posture-risk score. Nothing is randomised, and nothing is chosen to steer you towards a particular product.

    1. Ergo Budget Ratio™ (allocation)

    Your budget is divided across six workstation categories. The baseline split is chair 35%, desk 25%, monitor 20%, keyboard 10%, lighting 5%, cables 5%. Additive modifiers then shift weight based on your profile:

    • Lower back pain: chair +5%, desk +2%, keyboard −2%, lighting −2%, cables −3%
    • Neck / shoulder pain: monitor +6%, chair +2%, desk −3%, keyboard −2%, cables −3%
    • Wrist / hand pain: keyboard +6%, desk +1%, chair −2%, lighting −2%, cables −3%
    • 8+ desk hours: chair +3%, desk +2%, cables −5%
    • Video editing: monitor +6%, lighting +2%, cables −4%, desk −4%

    No category may fall below 1% of the budget. The set is then renormalised by water-filling so the six ratios always total exactly 100%, which is why a category can gain slightly more than its stated modifier.

    2. Cost-Per-Comfort-Hour™ (CPCH)

    CPCH expresses what an hour of comfortable work costs once your purchase is spread over its service life:

    CPCH = budget ÷ (desk hours × 365 × 5)

    Desk hours are banded as 3, 5, 7 or 9 hours per day. The 365 × 5 term assumes five working days a week for a five-year service life, so the result is the amortised hourly cost of the whole setup — useful for comparing a cheap chair you replace every two years against an expensive one you keep for ten.

    3. Posture Risk score

    The risk band is a simple additive score, not a clinical assessment:

    • Chair spend under 150 / 250 / 400 adds 4 / 2 / 1 points (first tier only)
    • Desk hours add 0, 1, 2 or 3 points for under 4, 4–6, 6–8 and 8+ hours
    • Each selected pain area adds 1 point; “No pain” adds none

    Scores of 0–2 read Low, 3–5 Moderate and 6 or more High. The score measures how under-resourced your planned setup is relative to your exposure — it says nothing about your health.

    4. Anthropometric height reference

    From your height we derive two reference working heights using anthropometric ratios common in workstation guidance:

    • Seated working height ≈ stature × 0.41
    • Standing working height ≈ stature × 0.63

    These are planning references for where your elbows should rest, not measurements of your body. Real ergonomic setup should always be adjusted to your own comfort.

    Standards we reference

    The ratios above are modelled on the anthropometric and postural guidance published in:

    • OSHA Computer Workstations eTool — US guidance on workstation components, seated and standing working heights, monitor placement and neutral wrist posture.
    • ISO 9241-5 — Ergonomics of human–system interaction, Part 5: Workstation layout and postural requirements, the international reference for fitting a workstation to the operator.
    • ANSI/HFES 100 — US human factors engineering standard for computer workstations, referenced for seated adjustability ranges.

    These standards are cited as background for the ratios used. This calculator is an independent educational tool: it is not certified to, audited against, or endorsed by OSHA, ISO or ANSI, and following its output does not guarantee compliance with any standard or regulation.
