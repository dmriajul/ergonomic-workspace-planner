The short answer

Fill in your details above and we will build your personal Ergonomic Desk Setup Budget. Not sure where to start? A split that works for most people is 35% for an ergonomic chair, 25% for a desk and 20% for a monitor. The last 20% covers your keyboard, lighting and cables. We base those shares on OSHA standards for workstation setup, then tilt them toward the part of your body that needs the most help. If your lower back hurts, more goes to the chair. If your neck and shoulders hurt, more goes to the monitor. If your wrists hurt, more goes to the keyboard.

    Methodology

    How We Calculate Your Budget

    Every number you see comes from three simple formulas. They use your answers and nothing else. No part of it is random, and no brand pays to be placed in your plan.

    1. Ergo Budget Ratio (how we split your money)

    We split your budget across six parts of a desk setup. The starting split is chair 35%, desk 25%, monitor 20%, keyboard 10%, lighting 5%, cables 5%. Then we move a few points around, based on what you told us:

    • Lower back pain: chair +5%, desk +2%, keyboard −2%, lighting −2%, cables −3%
    • Neck or shoulder pain: monitor +6%, chair +2%, desk −3%, keyboard −2%, cables −3%
    • Wrist or hand pain: keyboard +6%, desk +1%, chair −2%, lighting −2%, cables −3%
    • 8 or more desk hours: chair +3%, desk +2%, cables −5%
    • Video editing: monitor +6%, lighting +2%, cables −4%, desk −4%

    No part can ever drop below 1% of your budget. After the points move, we even the six shares out again so they always add up to exactly 100%. That is why one part can end up a little above or below the numbers in the list.

    2. Cost-Per-Comfort-Hour (CPCH)

    This number answers one question: what does one comfortable hour at your desk cost you? We spread the price of your setup across the hours you will use it:

    CPCH = budget ÷ (desk hours × 365 × 5)

    We count your desk hours as 3, 5, 7 or 9 a day. The 365 × 5 part means five days a week for five years of use. That gives you the real cost of one comfortable hour, which makes comparing gear much easier. A cheap chair you replace every two years can cost more per hour than a good one you keep for ten.

    3. Posture Risk score

    This one is just a tally. It is not a medical test. We add up a few points to see where your plan may be thin:

    • Chair spend under 150, 250 or 400 adds 4, 2 or 1 points (only the first one that fits counts)
    • Desk hours add 0, 1, 2 or 3 points for under 4, 4 to 6, 6 to 8 and 8+ hours
    • Each pain area you pick adds 1 point, and “No pain” adds none

    A score of 0 to 2 shows as Low, 3 to 5 as Moderate, and 6 or more as High. The score only shows whether your planned setup is keeping up with the hours you work. It tells you nothing about your health.

    4. Height reference

    Your height gives us two working heights to aim for. We use the same body ratios that appear in standard workstation guidance:

    • Seated working height ≈ stature × 0.41
    • Standing working height ≈ stature × 0.63

    Treat these as starting points for where your elbows should rest. They are not exact measurements of your body, so fine-tune your setup until it feels right for you.

    Standards we reference

    The body ratios above follow the posture and body measurement guidance published by:

    • OSHA Computer Workstations eTool: US guidance on desk parts, seated and standing working heights, where to put your monitor, and how to keep your wrists straight.
    • ISO 9241-5: Ergonomics of human system interaction, Part 5: Workstation layout and postural requirements, the global reference for fitting a desk to the person using it.
    • ANSI/HFES 100: the US human factors engineering standard for computer workstations, which we use for seated adjustability ranges.

    We mention these standards only to show where the ratios come from. This calculator is an independent learning tool. It is not certified by, audited against, or endorsed by OSHA, ISO or ANSI, and following its results does not mean your setup meets any standard or regulation.
